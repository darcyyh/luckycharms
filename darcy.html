I am definitely a superstitious person, but the first thing that comes to mind when I think of a lucky charm is sort of the opposite. When my family visited Paris in 2024, I split off from them and explored different art museums every day. While potentially cliché, Vincent Van Gogh is one of my absolute favorite artists. Luckily for me, the Musée d’Orsay was hosting a Van Gogh exhibition focusing on the final months of his life. It was there I was able to see his final painting ever—the one he was working on the same day he died—“Tree Roots.” It was quite emotional for me. I stood in front of it until my feet hurt. I purchased a small magnet of it at the gift shop.

Since then I’ve stayed in two different dorm rooms, two different bedrooms at my parent’s houses, and just recently decorated my current apartment. The magnet has been taped up above my pillow in each of them. It’s also my phone wallpaper. I like seeing it repeatedly, even as the constant exposure makes me eventually stop noticing it each time. I find it an aesthetically beautiful painting, and the context only gives it more gravity.

It’s maybe a bit morbid for an object I center so significantly. If I sit and think about it for too long it’ll make me sad, but in the quick glances I afford it daily, I am only appreciating its beauty. I always have the option to reflect on it if I’d like, it’ll wait for me to get into the mindset to do so. I think that’s what draws me to it so much. The magnet has followed me through so many major changes, and its consistent location anchors me a bit. In that sense, I think it qualifies as a lucky charm or habit—seeing the painting, in whatever form it’s in, brings me a little comfort.
